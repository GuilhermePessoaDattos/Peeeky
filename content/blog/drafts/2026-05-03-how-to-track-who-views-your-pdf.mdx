You sent the PDF. Now what?

You're refreshing your inbox. Checking if they replied. Wondering whether they even opened the attachment. Sound familiar?

Most professionals have no idea what happens to a document after they hit send. You might get an email confirmation days later, or more likely — nothing. That gap is where deals are lost, follow-ups are mistimed, and momentum dies.

This post walks you through how to actually track who views your PDF, what they read, and what to do with that data.

---

## Why Standard PDF Attachments Don't Give You Visibility

When you send a PDF via email as an attachment, the file lives on the recipient's computer. Once it's downloaded, it's completely outside your control. You have zero insight into:

- Whether they opened it
- Which pages they spent the most time on
- Whether they shared it with someone else
- How many times they came back to review it

Some email tools offer "read receipts," but these are unreliable — most email clients block them — and they only tell you the email was opened, not that the document was actually read.

To get real PDF viewing data, you need a different approach.

---

## The Right Way: Trackable Document Links

Instead of attaching a PDF directly, you upload it to a document-sharing platform and send a **trackable link**. The link points to a hosted version of your document, and every interaction is logged.

This gives you:

- **View notifications** — get an alert the moment someone opens your document
- **Page-by-page analytics** — see which sections they actually read and how long they spent on each
- **Viewer identification** — know who opened it, not just that someone did
- **Multi-viewer tracking** — if they forward the link, you see every viewer separately
- **Return visits** — know when someone comes back for a second or third look

This is the same approach used by sales teams, founders raising capital, and consultants sharing proposals.

## Step-by-Step: How to Track Who Views Your PDF

### Step 1: Upload Your PDF to a Tracking Platform

Tools like [Peeeky](https://peeeky.com) let you upload your PDF and instantly generate a shareable link. The original file is hosted securely — you control access at all times.

### Step 2: Enable Viewer Identification

Most platforms let you require viewers to enter their email before accessing the document. This means every time someone opens the link, you know exactly who it was — not just "someone" viewed your PDF.

You can also choose to keep the document open (no email gate) if you want frictionless access, and still capture anonymous viewing data.

### Step 3: Send the Link Instead of the Attachment

Replace the PDF attachment in your email with the trackable link. For investors, clients, or prospects, you can embed it naturally:

> "Here's the deck I mentioned — [link]. Happy to walk through it on a call once you've had a chance to review."

### Step 4: Monitor the Analytics Dashboard

Once the link is live, you'll see real-time data on:

- **Who viewed it** (name + email, if ID is enabled)
- **When** they opened it (timestamp)
- **How long** they spent on each page (heatmap-style breakdown)
- **How many times** they opened it total

If someone has viewed page 4 (pricing) three times but skipped the intro entirely, that tells you something specific about where they are in the decision process.

### Step 5: Follow Up at the Right Moment

This is where PDF tracking pays off.

Instead of following up on an arbitrary schedule, you follow up based on **behavior signals**:

- They just opened the document → reach out while it's top of mind
- They spent 3 minutes on the pricing page → lead with commercial terms in your follow-up
- They haven't opened it after 4 days → send a gentle bump

Timing follow-ups this way dramatically increases response rates. You're not guessing — you're responding to intent.

## What About Privacy?

Tracking raises a fair question: is this ethical?

Yes — and here's why.

You created the document. You own it. Knowing who accesses it and when is a natural extension of sharing it, the same way a website tracks page visits. You're not tracking anything the recipient didn't consent to by clicking a link you shared with them.

Most professional contexts — fundraising, sales, legal negotiations — involve documents where understanding engagement is expected and legitimate.

That said, you should always use this data to provide value (better follow-ups, more relevant conversations), not to pressure or surveil in ways that make recipients uncomfortable.
