## Proposed response

---

A few gaps I kept running into when we were raising:

**1. Analytics that stop at "opened."**
Most tools tell you someone opened the deck. What you actually need is: did they share it internally? (Multiple opens from the same IP in the same afternoon is almost always forwarding.) Did they come back on a different day? Which slides consumed the most time vs. which they skipped in two seconds? The delta between "they spent 8 minutes on the market slide" and "they spent 8 seconds" is the difference between a warm follow-up and a dead one.

**2. No way to ask a question mid-deck.**
Investors read async, usually late at night. If they hit a slide they don't understand — the unit economics, the go-to-market thesis — they close the tab rather than email you a question. A lightweight Q&A layer attached to the doc itself (not a separate thread) would capture a lot of signal that currently disappears.

**3. Version chaos.**
You update the deck three times during a raise. Half your links are still pointing at v1. You have no idea which investors saw the "team slide we fixed" versus the embarrassing earlier one. Version control with link persistence is genuinely underbuilt in this space.

**4. The "sent, now what?" silence.**
Tools are good at tracking opens but they don't help you think through what the data means — e.g., "they opened it 4 times in the last two days, here's a suggested follow-up script based on which slides they lingered on." The data is there; the interpretation layer isn't.

None of this is technically hard. It's mostly that the incumbents built v1 of these features and then stopped iterating. The gap is real and worth building into.
