## Why this thread

Active HN thread debating whether the fundraising process itself is broken — warm intros mattering more than traction, VCs wanting optionality over conviction, AI crowding out other categories. The "warm intros" angle opens a natural discussion about how founders can compensate by making their materials do more work: better-signaling decks, engagement data as a proxy for interest, controlling the narrative through what you send and how you send it. Peeeky is relevant but doesn't need naming — the valuable contribution is practical advice about what founders can actually control.

## Proposed response

---

Both, but that framing treats them as mutually exclusive.

The system does have structural problems — warm intros concentrate deal flow in a narrow network, and a lot of "nos" have nothing to do with the startup. But I'd push back on using that as the explanation for any individual raise going badly. The founders who compensate most effectively tend to do a few things differently:

**They treat the deck like a sales asset, not a credential.**
A PDF attachment or a DocSend link that bounces back zero signal is a missed opportunity. Every interaction with your materials is data — which slides stopped them, whether they came back, whether it got forwarded internally (multi-viewer sessions with fast reads usually means someone is circulating). That data changes how you prioritize and how you follow up.

**They control the second meeting narrative before the first one ends.**
The question "can you send me the deck?" is often where deals quietly die. If you send a static PDF and then wait, you've handed control to a process you can't see. If you send a tracked link and get back signal that they've been back twice in two days, you have a reason to follow up that isn't just checking in — it's responding to observable interest.

**They accept that warm intros are a constraint, not a gate.**
The constraint is real but not absolute. Cold outreach with strong materials, specific fund thesis alignment, and timing awareness (follow recent portfolio company announcements, sector momentum) does convert. It's a lower base rate, but at enough volume with good targeting it works.

The structural problem isn't going away. The practical response is to make your materials and follow-up process compensate for the lack of network advantage.
